Absorbance

The spectrophotometer is equipped with a CCD-array and is capable of measuring wavelengths in the interval 305 nm to 1050 nm. A fibre-optics cable 1.5 meters long transmits the light from the source to the device.

It is possible to measure absorbance, since it is possible to send the light of a built-in halogen lamp into an external cell holder with another fibre-optics cable.

The spectrophotometer can only scan the spectrum. It cannot measure at a specified wavelength. But a scan of 1024 values is measured in no more than 2 seconds (1 second). This way you get the entire spectrum on the screen. This sounds very good.

A measuring does take some time however since CCD are not without noise, i.e. they measure light even in darkness:

Emission:

  1. Measure background (if the measuring time is changes, you have to measure the background again).
  2. Measure Emission, the background (column 2 in the table) is subtracted.

Absorption:

  1. Measure reference, i.e. measure on a cell containing water. The background is subtracted and the reference spectrum is written to column 3 of the table.
  2. Measure absorbance. The absorbance is calculated using measured values and the reference.

Comments:

CCD's and fibre-optics cables are the future, there are some reservations about the device.

  • A continual spectrum i.e. from a flashlight bulb or the built-in halogen lamp is not measured as continual but with strange peaks. This cannot be right!
  • The spectrum of built-in lamp only go from about 450 nm to 900 nm, which means that you can only measure absorbance in this area.
  • There are problems with some of the codes for serial communication.
